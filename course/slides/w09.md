# 1. PID

---

# What each term buys, and costs

$$ u(t) = K_p e(t) + K_i \int_0^t e(\tau)\,d\tau + K_d \frac{de}{dt} $$

| Term | Buys | Costs |
|---|---|---|
| **P** | Speed of response | Steady-state offset; oscillation |
| **I** | Kills steady-state offset | Windup, overshoot, sluggishness |
| **D** | Damping | **Amplifies sensor noise** |

---

# D on a noisy encoder cooks a gearmotor

Differentiating a noisy signal amplifies the noise.

On a mechanical steering column that is not just *seen* — it is **actuated**.
Audible chatter, and real current draw.

- Filter the derivative
- Or start at PD with a small D
- Or use derivative-on-**measurement**, so a setpoint step
  does not produce a spike

---

# Where the loop lives

**Not in ROS.** Two reasons.

**Timing.** Linux cannot promise 200 Hz. A loop that *usually* makes its
deadline oscillates unpredictably under load.

**Survival.** If the USB link drops, a loop running in ROS is *gone*.
A loop on the Teensy is still there — and can centre the wheels and
de-energise, which is what failsafe row 1 requires.

# 2. Path tracking

---

# A different problem entirely

There is no setpoint in the same sense.

There is a **path**, and the vehicle is somewhere near it.

---

# Pure pursuit

Pick a point on the path a lookahead distance $L_d$ ahead.
Compute the arc from here to there.

$$ \kappa = \frac{2\sin\alpha}{L_d} \qquad \delta = \arctan(\kappa L) $$

- No error term
- No gains
- No integrator

**Pure geometry** — which is why it cannot ask for a manoeuvre
the car cannot perform.

---

# Lookahead is the one real knob

| $L_d$ | Behaviour |
|---|---|
| Too short | Tracks tightly, oscillates, chases every wiggle |
| Too long | Smooth and stable, but **cuts corners** |

---

# What "Regulated" adds

- **Curvature regulation** — slow down in tight turns
- **Proximity regulation** — slow down near obstacles
- **Velocity-scaled lookahead** — longer when fast, shorter when slow

```yaml
desired_linear_vel: 0.6       # well under the 1.4 m/s cap
lookahead_dist: 0.8
min_lookahead_dist: 0.4
max_lookahead_dist: 1.2
use_velocity_scaled_lookahead_dist: true
min_turning_radius: 1.6
```

---

# Two parameters that cannot both be true

```yaml
use_rotate_to_heading: false
allow_reversing: true
```

Set both `true` and **Nav2 refuses to start.**

Rotate-to-heading means *"stop and spin to face the right way"* —
which this vehicle cannot do at all.

So `false` is not a preference. It is the only legal value for a car —
and it is what makes `allow_reversing` available.

# 3. The constraint reaches the goal checker

---

# These look sloppy. They are geometry.

```yaml
goal_checker:
  xy_goal_tolerance: 0.6
  yaw_goal_tolerance: 0.6
```

`min_turning_radius: 1.6` = R_min 1.52 m + margin.

A car with that radius and no in-place rotation **cannot reduce a
0.75 m pose error.** It can only loop past and retry.

0.6 m is ~40% of the turning radius — achievable in one pass.

---

# F-N3: the car that orbited forever

At 0.25 m tolerance the vehicle arrived within 0.75 m, then circled:

```
(-4.19,-1.46) (-5.41, 0.43) (-2.28, 0.81) (-4.51,-1.35) ...
```

**It never converged. It never failed.**

No error. No timeout. No diagnostic.
Just a car driving in circles around a point it could not reach.
