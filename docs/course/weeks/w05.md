## 1. The non-holonomic constraint

A differential-drive robot can rotate in place. A car cannot. The formal statement of that
difference is a **non-holonomic constraint**: the vehicle has three degrees of freedom in
configuration space (*x*, *y*, θ) but only **two** control inputs (speed and steering angle), and it
cannot translate sideways at all.

$$ \dot{x}\sin\theta - \dot{y}\cos\theta = 0 $$

The constraint restricts *velocities*, not positions — a car can reach any parking space, it just
cannot go there directly. Everything awkward about planning for MRider descends from this one line.

---

## 2. The bicycle model

For planning, four wheels collapse to two: the front pair to one steered wheel at the centre of the
front axle, the rear pair to one driven wheel at the centre of the rear axle.

```
            ↑ x (forward)
            |
    ┌───────────────┐
    │       ⊙ ← front wheel, steered by δ
    │       ┆       │
    │       ┆ L     │      L = wheelbase = 0.63 m
    │       ┆       │      δ = steering angle
    │       ⊗ ← rear wheel  (base_link is here)
    └───────────────┘
```

Held at a constant steering angle δ, the vehicle traces a circle whose centre lies on the extension
of the rear axle, at distance *R*:

$$ \tan\delta = \frac{L}{R} \qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad R = \frac{L}{\tan\delta} $$

$$ \omega = \frac{v}{R} = \frac{v\tan\delta}{L} $$

**Read the second equation carefully.** If *v* = 0 then ω = 0 for *any* δ whatsoever. This is the
formal reason your Lab 2 square driver had to keep `linear.x` non-zero through its turns.

### Why real Ackermann geometry is not quite this

On a real vehicle the inner and outer front wheels trace circles of different radii, so they must
point at **different angles** — that is what an Ackermann linkage mechanically arranges.

The bicycle model uses one angle for both. This is an approximation, and at MRider's scale a good
one, but it is a live question in Lab 5: **which wheel does the mechanism's 22.5° limit actually
apply to?**

---

## 3. The number that shapes this course

MRider's front wheels are limited to **δ_max = 22.5°**:

$$ R_{min} = \frac{L}{\tan\delta_{max}} = \frac{0.63}{\tan 22.5°} = \frac{0.63}{0.4142} = 1.52\ \text{m} $$

The vehicle **cannot** turn inside a 1.52 m radius. Not "prefers not to" — its wheels do not point
that far.

Where it shows up:

| Where | Parameter | Value |
|---|---|---|
| `nav2_params.yaml`, planner | `minimum_turning_radius` | 1.6 |
| `nav2_params.yaml`, controller | `min_turning_radius` | 1.6 |
| `nav2_params.yaml`, goal checker | `xy_goal_tolerance` | 0.6 m |

Both radii are R_min plus a small margin. The goal tolerance is 0.6 m rather than the more usual
0.25 m **because of geometry**: a vehicle with a 1.52 m turning circle physically cannot make fine
positional corrections near a goal. Ask it to and it orbits — which is exactly what happened during
bring-up, and is recorded in `software.md §4.4`.

!!! danger "Both numbers derive from values nobody has measured"

    `0.63` and `22.5°` are both estimates. The dimensions file says so. When the Chassis track
    measures the real vehicle in November, **both parameters must be re-derived together** — and
    [Lab 5 Part 5](../labs/lab5.md) has you rehearse exactly that, by setting the wheelbase to 0.75
    and watching what happens.

## 4. What this costs a planner

A planner that assumes free rotation produces paths this vehicle cannot drive. That is not a tuning
problem — no controller can follow an infeasible path.

This is why **ADR-SW2 closed the way it did**: NavFn plans as though the robot were a free-turning
point, so it routinely returned undrivable paths, and DWB samples a velocity space that at R_min is
mostly unreachable. Both were dropped **on geometry, without a trial run**.

What replaced them:

| Role | Plugin | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Planner | `SmacPlannerHybrid` | Searches (x, y, **heading**) with Reeds-Shepp curves bounded by the turning radius. Cannot express an undrivable path |
| Controller | `RegulatedPurePursuit` | Curvature-aware, and `allow_reversing: true` |

The verified result is worth knowing before Week 12: a goal **2.5 m directly behind** the vehicle —
geometrically impossible forward-only at a 1.6 m radius — was reached by reversing 2.1 m in a
straight line. **Before the planner swap, that manoeuvre had no solution at all.**
