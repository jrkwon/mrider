## What you are graded on

Two rubrics are applied today, and they ask different questions.

| | Asks | Read from |
|---|---|---|
| **Final demo & presentation** (20%) | What does the integrated vehicle do, and do you understand it? | Today, in the room |
| **Team deliverable** (25%) | Is each track's subsystem in the repository, with its evidence? | Your fork, as it stands at the start of class |

The standard is the one the course started with: **honest failure outscores a lucky success.** A
team whose vehicle maps the hallway and cannot yet reach a Nav2 goal, and can show precisely why
with evidence, has done better work than a team whose vehicle reached a goal once and cannot say
what made it work.

Overclaiming costs points under Presentation. "It navigates autonomously" after one successful goal
in one room is not a finding. "One goal reached in four attempts; the three failures were …" is.

## The presentation

**15 minutes, then 5 of questions. Everyone speaks.** The structure is the final report's:

1. **Approach** (2 min) — what you set out to build
2. **Implementation** (3 min) — what you actually built, and what differs from the design documents
3. **Results** (4 min) — the acceptance gates: number, procedure, conditions, trials
4. **Failure analysis** (4 min) — the heart of it. What broke, which subsystem, what evidence
5. **Reproduction** (2 min) — what another team needs in order to repeat it

Questions go to individuals, and may be about a part of the system that is not yours. *"The AI
wrote it"* and *"that was the other track"* are not answers
([syllabus](../syllabus.md#academic-integrity)).

## The demo

**15 minutes at the vehicle.** Show the highest step of the [Merge 4 ladder](w14.md#merge-4-full-integration)
you have evidence for, and the steps below it first:

1. **Teleop**, with the RC override and the E-stop demonstrated
2. **A hallway map**, built live or loaded, with the loop it closed
3. **A Nav2 goal**, on that map

A step your vehicle has not passed in rehearsal is not attempted for the first time in front of the
class.

> [!CAUTION]
> **The safety gate comes before the rubric**
>
> The [staged bring-up](../../design/safety.md#6-bring-up-protocol-staged-wheels-off-first) is:
> bench before vehicle, wheels-off before wheels-on, walking pace before anything faster. A team
> that skipped a stage does not lose 10 points. **It does not drive.** It presents, and it
> demonstrates on stands.

### Roles

| | |
|---|---|
| **Operator** | Holds the RC transmitter, thumbs on the sticks, for the whole run |
| **Spotter** | Hand on the E-stop. Watches the vehicle and the people, not the screen |
| **Engineer** | At the laptop. The only one who sends commands |

### Before the first run

- [ ] Clear area marked; everyone not in a role is behind the line
- [ ] E-stop pressed once: traction cuts and the vehicle reverts to STOCK. Sticks centred, hands
      off, then reset
- [ ] RC override taken once under a laptop command: it preempts on both axes
- [ ] `MITT_VEHICLE=<n>` sourced in every terminal; `vehicle:=<n>` on every launch;
      `use_sim_time:=false`
- [ ] No `WRONG VEHICLE` block in the Nav2 terminal
- [ ] `/mitt/dbw/status` at ≥ 50 Hz
- [ ] Map loaded and localization converged, if you are showing steps 2 or 3
- [ ] For a goal that needs reverse: `/scan` shows the space behind the vehicle in RViz

### Each run

- [ ] Course clear, said aloud
- [ ] Call and response: *"Commanding." / "Ready." / "Clear." / "Go."*
- [ ] Afterwards, before anything else: the outcome written down. Time, interventions, anomalies
- [ ] Vehicle to STOCK between runs

### When it goes wrong

It may. **Stop, say what you saw, and say what you think it is.** A team that halts a run with the
E-stop at the right moment has demonstrated the thing the safety rubric is for. A team that lets a
run continue to see whether it recovers has not.

### Teardown

- [ ] E-stop latched, MUX de-energized, pack disconnected
- [ ] Bags and logs copied off the laptop **before** it leaves the room

## After today

`main` ends the semester with one `firmware/`, one `mitt_hardware` and one `mitt_bench`
([Teams §Where your work lives](../teams.md#where-your-work-lives)). For each, the implementation
with the strongest acceptance-gate evidence is merged from its team's fork with its commit history.
Which one is merged is not a grade.

Two things every team can still contribute, whichever implementation is chosen:

- **Your measured numbers.** `config/vehicles/vehicle<n>.yaml` is already on `main`. The
  *(measure during bring-up)* rows in `docs/build/` that you filled in are not, until you send them.
- **What the documents got wrong.** These pages were written before anyone had built the vehicle.
  Where a procedure was wrong, a pull request that says what actually happened is the most durable
  thing you leave behind.
