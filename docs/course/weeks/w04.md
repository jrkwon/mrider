## Lecture

### 1. URDF and Xacro

A URDF describes a robot as **links** (rigid bodies with visual, collision, and inertial
properties) joined by **joints** (fixed, revolute, continuous, prismatic).

Written literally, a URDF is repetitive and unmaintainable. **Xacro** adds properties, macros, and
expressions, expanded before anything reads it:

```bash
xacro src/mitt_description/urdf/mitt.urdf.xacro > /tmp/mitt.urdf
```

!!! quote "MRider's URDF contains no literal dimensions. That is the whole design."

    Every value comes from `config/mitt_dimensions.yaml`, whose header reads:

    > **NOTHING IN THIS FILE HAS BEEN MEASURED.** Every value is an ESTIMATE derived from the
    > vendor's published overall dimensions.

    Not an apology — a mechanism. In November the Chassis track replaces those numbers with measured
    ones, and **no geometry anywhere else has to change.**

    The trap being avoided is documented: a predecessor's URDF carried `chassis_mass = 300 kg` for a
    ride-on car, and its controller config listed a wheelbase contradicting its own URDF. Nobody
    noticed, because in simulation nothing complains.

### 2. TF2 — the transform tree

Every sensor reading arrives in *some* frame. TF2 answers "where is this, relative to that?" at a
given time.

MRider's tree, and who owns each edge:

| Transform | Published by | Kind |
|---|---|---|
| `map → odom` | `slam_toolbox` | dynamic — the drift correction |
| `odom → base_link` | `robot_localization` EKF | dynamic — the local pose estimate |
| `base_link → laser_link`, `→ camera_link`, … | `robot_state_publisher`, from the URDF | static |

**Ownership is exclusive, and enforced.** The Ackermann controller is configured with
`enable_odom_tf: false` precisely so the EKF is the *sole* publisher of `odom → base_link`.

!!! danger "Two publishers for one transform"

    The result is a robot that jitters, and it is genuinely hard to diagnose — nothing errors,
    both publishers are behaving correctly, and the tree looks complete in `view_frames`.

`base_link` sits at the **rear axle centre, on the ground plane** (REP-105). That is why
`base_link_height` and `wheel_radius` are both 0.09 in the dimensions file — not a coincidence, a
derivation.

!!! tip "`tf2_echo` needs `use_sim_time:=true`"

    Without it, `tf2_echo` uses wall-clock time while every transform is stamped with simulation
    time. You get extrapolation errors that look like a broken tree. This costs people an hour the
    first time; it need not cost you one.

### 3. From description to motion

The chain that turns a text file into a moving robot:

```
mitt.urdf.xacro
   │ xacro expands
   ▼
/robot_description  ──▶ robot_state_publisher ──▶ TF (base_link → *)
   │
   │ ros_gz_sim create
   ▼
model spawned in Gazebo
   │  <gazebo><plugin gz_ros2_control>
   ▼
controller_manager  ── loads ──▶ joint_state_broadcaster
       (inside the Gazebo process)      ackermann_steering_controller
```

Two things about that diagram are load-bearing:

**The controller manager runs *inside* the Gazebo process.** That is what `gz_ros2_control` does,
and it is why the CycloneDDS problem in `setup_env.sh` is specifically about service *responses*
crossing that boundary.

**Actuation does not cross the ROS–Gazebo bridge.** Look at `mitt_sim/config/gz_bridge.yaml`: every
entry is `GZ_TO_ROS` — clock, scan, IMU, camera. Nothing in that file commands the vehicle. Commands
reach the joints through `ros2_control` inside the simulator.

!!! success "This is ADR-SW4, and it is the point of the whole twin"

    The identical controller stack runs in simulation and on hardware, **differing only in the
    `hardware_interface` plugin.** `mitt_controllers.yaml` is used unchanged in both.

    Its header says what follows: *"If a value here needs to be different on the real vehicle, that
    is a signal something is wrong with the model, not a reason to fork the file."*

    The plugin for real hardware — `mitt_hardware` — **does not exist yet.** It is the Software
    track's keystone deliverable, and Merge 3 in Week 13 exists to prove it.

### 4. Ackermann kinematics

The bicycle model: front wheels collapse to one steered wheel, rear wheels to one driven wheel.

$$ \tan\delta = \frac{L}{R} \qquad R = \frac{L}{\tan\delta} \qquad \omega = \frac{v\tan\delta}{L} $$

Two consequences, both of which you have already felt:

- **`v = 0` ⟹ `ω = 0`.** A car cannot rotate in place. Your Lab 2 square driver had to keep
  `linear.x` non-zero through its turns.
- **Steering is bounded, so the turning radius is bounded.**

$$ R_{min} = \frac{L}{\tan\delta_{max}} = \frac{0.63}{\tan 22.5°} = 1.52\ \text{m} $$

That number appears **twice** in `nav2_params.yaml` — `minimum_turning_radius` on the planner,
`min_turning_radius` on the controller — and it is why `xy_goal_tolerance` is 0.6 m rather than the
usual 0.25 m. A vehicle with a 1.52 m turning circle cannot make fine positional corrections near a
goal. Ask it to and it orbits.

**Lab 5 has you measure it**, and find that theory and measurement disagree by about 2%.

### 5. The third silent failure

| Week | Failure | Symptom |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | `ROS_DOMAIN_ID` mismatch | Nothing visible at all |
| 3 | QoS incompatibility | Connection silently never forms |
| **4** | **Wrong sensor extrinsic** | **Everything runs, and the output is quietly wrong** |

The third is the worst. Nothing errors. The LiDAR publishes, TF is complete, SLAM runs, and the map
is wrong — because every component is healthy and is faithfully computing the consequences of one
bad number.

MRider's mapping acceptance gate is that repeated observations of the same wall agree within **10 cm**
over a 30 m loop. Lab 4 has you introduce a 5 cm error and watch that gate fail.
