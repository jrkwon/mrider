## Lecture

### 1. Odometry: integrating your way to a position

Wheel odometry integrates measured wheel rotation and steering angle through the bicycle model:

$$ \dot{x} = v\cos\theta \qquad \dot{y} = v\sin\theta \qquad \dot{\theta} = \frac{v\tan\delta}{L} $$

Two constants convert raw counts into those quantities: `meters_per_tick`, and the steering
counts-to-radians mapping from the absolute sensor.

!!! success "MRider refuses to derive `meters_per_tick` from specifications"

    The calibration procedure is a **roll-out**: drive or push the vehicle a measured straight
    distance *L*, record the tick delta, and compute `meters_per_tick = L / Δticks`. Three times,
    averaged.

    This is authoritative **because it bypasses guessing.** You never need the gear ratio, the
    quadrature decode factor, or the tyre diameter under load — all of them fold into one measured
    number.

    Which is also why [finding F7](../../design/adr-dbw-architecture-review.md) can forbid inheriting
    a PPR figure without endangering anything. The source project's firmware says 52 PPR, its own
    BOM says 16. **Measure yours.** The roll-out makes the resulting distance safe either way.

### 2. Why it drifts

Integration accumulates error with no correction. Every source is systematic, not random:

| Error | Effect |
|---|---|
| Wheel slip | Rotation without ground motion — always over-estimates distance |
| Tyre deformation under load | Effective radius changes; MRider carries 6 kg on a 10 kg vehicle |
| Gearbox backlash | The encoder is upstream of it and cannot see it |
| Steering angle error | Heading error, which grows *unbounded* with distance |

Heading error is the dangerous one. A 1° heading bias puts you 0.35 m off after 20 m — and that
error keeps growing whether or not you keep making it.

!!! danger "MRider's measurement is narrower than a generic robot's"

    Both rear motors are wired **in parallel to a single driver channel**, and only **one** carries
    an encoder ([ADR C](../../design/dbw.md)). The measurement is taken at one motor shaft.

    So in a turn, when the two rear wheels genuinely travel different distances, **one of them is
    simply not observed.** Its speed is inferred, not measured.

    This is a documented, deliberately accepted trade — see the diagram in
    [M5](../../learn/m5-slam.md) — and it is the reason the next section is not optional.

### 3. The EKF

An Extended Kalman Filter fuses several noisy estimates into one better estimate, weighted by how
much each is trusted, propagating uncertainty as it goes.

Open `mitt_localization/config/ekf_indoor.yaml`. Four decisions in it are worth reading closely.

**Fuse velocities, not pose.**

```yaml
odom0_config: [false, false, false,   # x, y, z        <- NOT fused
               false, false, false,   # roll, pitch, yaw
               true,  true,  false,   # vx, vy, vz     <- fused
               false, false, true,    # vroll, vpitch, vyaw
               false, false, false]
```

> *"Integrated wheel position drifts without bound and there is no absolute reference indoors to
> correct it."*

Feeding the EKF an already-drifted pose would have it fuse the drift as though it were evidence.

**Take the IMU's yaw, not its accelerations.**

> *"On a vibrating ride-on chassis they are mostly drivetrain buzz."*

A sensor axis you *can* read is not automatically one you *should* fuse.

**`imu0_relative: true`** — relative to the first measurement, because no magnetometer heading
reference is trusted indoors, near steel and motors.

**One EKF, and no `navsat_transform`.** B-MROVER's magnetic declination was set for **Lisbon**,
which would silently rotate the global frame. Rather than fix a number that cannot be verified here,
the indoor profile drops the GNSS EKF entirely.

!!! quote "That last one is the habit worth stealing"

    The choice was not between a right number and a wrong number. It was between an unverifiable
    number and **removing the thing that needed it.** Deleting the dependency was cheaper and
    honest; a plausible-looking wrong declination would have been neither.

### 4. Who owns which transform

| Transform | Owner |
|---|---|
| `map → odom` | `slam_toolbox` |
| `odom → base_link` | `robot_localization` EKF |
| `base_link → *` | `robot_state_publisher` |

The Ackermann controller runs with `enable_odom_tf: false` so the EKF is the **sole** publisher of
`odom → base_link`. Two publishers for one transform gives a jittering robot and no error message.

The acceptance gate this all serves: **≤ 2% translational drift over 20 m straight, and ≤ 5° heading
error after a closed 20 m figure-eight.** Nobody has measured either. That is the Simulation and
Validation track's `mitt_bench`.

## Teams

**Announced today.** Four teams of six: Electrical & Firmware, Chassis & Measurement, Simulation &
Validation, Software & Autonomy. See [Teams & Tracks](../teams.md).

### Charter workshop — in class, today

Write it while the other tracks are in the room. That is the point: three of the four sections are
about them.

Your charter, due **10/19** at `docs/course/charters/<track>.md`:

1. **Who owns what.** Every deliverable, with a *person's name* against it. Not "the team will."
2. **Definition of done.** For each deliverable, the observable evidence. *"The encoder is mounted"*
   is not evidence. *"The encoder reports monotonic counts through a full steering sweep, logged,
   plotted, committed"* is.
3. **What you need from other tracks, and by when.** With dates. Agree them today, out loud.
4. **Your risks.** Two or three things that could stop you, and what you would do instead.

!!! warning "The dependency section is the one that fails silently"

    Software needs measured geometry from Chassis. Simulation needs it too. Electrical needs the
    bench kits and the steering column dimensions. If those asks are not dated today, they will be
    discovered in Week 13, and Week 13 is too late.

### The standup, from today onward

The last 30 minutes of every session. Each team, briefly: **what moved, what is blocked, what you
need from whom.** This is where the participation grade is observed, and it is assessed on honest
reporting — including reporting that nothing moved.
